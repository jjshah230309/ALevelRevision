# Biology (AQA 7402) — Specification Audit

**Source:** Official AQA specification, *"AS and A-level Biology,"* Version 1.6, July 2026 (for teaching from 2015; AS/A-level exams from June 2016 onwards). Downloaded directly from aqa.org.uk on 6 September 2026 and read in full — every statement below is drawn from that document, not from general biology knowledge.

**Scope of this audit:** A-level only (not the AS-only route). Topics 1–4 are shared AS/A-level content; topics 5–8 are A-level only.

A dedicated AQA command-word glossary exists as a separate general document across their science subjects — it was **not** part of this specification PDF, so no command-word list is asserted here. Where words like "explain," "evaluate," "interpret," "appreciate" appear below, they're quoted directly from the spec, not drawn from an assumed glossary.

## 1. Qualification structure

Linear qualification: all A-level exams are sat at the end of Year 13, in a single May/June series.

| Paper | Content covered | Question types | Time | Marks | % of A-level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Topics 1–4 | 76 marks short/long answer + 15 marks extended response | 2 hours | 91 | 35% |
| 2 | Topics 5–8 | 76 marks short/long answer + 15 marks comprehension question | 2 hours | 91 | 35% |
| 3 | Topics 1–8 (synoptic) | 38 marks structured (incl. practical technique) + 15 marks critical analysis of experimental data + 25 marks essay (choice of 2 titles) | 2 hours | 78 | 30% |

Total scaled mark: 260 (Paper 1 = 91, Paper 2 = 91, Paper 3 = 78, all ×1 scaling).

A separate **practical endorsement** (Pass / Not Classified) is reported alongside the grade. It is teacher-assessed by direct observation and does **not** contribute marks to the written papers — but the 12 required practicals below are still examinable knowledge in the written papers.

## 2. Assessment objectives

Set by Ofqual; identical wording across all exam boards' Biology specifications.

- **AO1** — knowledge and understanding of scientific ideas, processes, techniques and procedures.
- **AO2** — application of that knowledge and understanding: in a theoretical context, in a practical context, when handling qualitative data, when handling quantitative data.
- **AO3** — analysis, interpretation and evaluation of scientific information, ideas and evidence (including issues), to make judgements/conclusions and to develop/refine practical design and procedures.

| AO | Paper 1 | Paper 2 | Paper 3 | Overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AO1 | 44–48% | 23–27% | 28–32% | 30–35% |
| AO2 | 30–34% | 52–56% | 35–39% | 40–45% |
| AO3 | 20–24% | 19–23% | 31–35% | 25–30% |

10% of the overall assessment carries mathematical skills at Level 2 or above. **At least 15%** of the overall assessment assesses practical knowledge, skills and understanding (this is examined in the written papers, separately from the practical endorsement).

## 3. Topic-by-topic content

### Topic 1 (spec 3.1) — Biological molecules
- **3.1.1 Monomers and polymers** — monomers/polymers defined; monosaccharides, amino acids, nucleotides as monomer examples; condensation vs hydrolysis reactions.
- **3.1.2 Carbohydrates** — monosaccharides (glucose, galactose, fructose); glycosidic bonds; disaccharides (maltose, sucrose, lactose) and their component monosaccharides; α- and β-glucose isomers; polysaccharides (glycogen, starch from α-glucose; cellulose from β-glucose) and structure–function relationships; Benedict's test (reducing/non-reducing sugars) and iodine test (starch).
- **3.1.3 Lipids** — triglycerides and phospholipids; ester bond formation; saturated/unsaturated fatty acid R-groups; phospholipid structure (phosphate-substituted fatty acid); emulsion test; relate structure to properties.
- **3.1.4 Proteins**
  - *3.1.4.1 General properties* — amino acid general structure (NH₂, COOH, R-group), the 20 common amino acids; peptide bonds, di-/polypeptides; hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bridges; primary/secondary/tertiary/quaternary structure and function; biuret test.
  - *3.1.4.2 Enzymes* — activation energy; induced-fit model; active site tertiary structure and substrate specificity; effects of enzyme concentration, substrate concentration, competitive/non-competitive inhibitor concentration, pH, temperature on reaction rate.
  - **Required practical 1**: effect of a named variable on the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction.
- **3.1.5 Nucleic acids**
  - *3.1.5.1 Structure of DNA and RNA* — DNA holds genetic information, RNA transfers it to ribosomes; nucleotide structure (pentose + phosphate + base); DNA bases (A, C, G, T) vs RNA bases (A, C, G, U); phosphodiester bonds; DNA double helix held by hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs; RNA as a short polynucleotide chain.
  - *3.1.5.2 DNA replication* — semi-conservative replication: unwinding of the double helix, breakage of hydrogen bonds, role of DNA helicase, base pairing of new nucleotides to template strands, role of DNA polymerase.
- **3.1.6 ATP** — ATP structure (ribose + adenine + 3 phosphates); hydrolysis to ADP + Pi by ATP hydrolase, coupled to energy-requiring reactions; resynthesis from ADP + Pi by ATP synthase during photosynthesis/respiration.
- **3.1.7 Water** — metabolite in condensation/hydrolysis reactions; solvent for metabolic reactions; high heat capacity (buffers temperature change); high latent heat of vaporisation (cooling with little water loss); strong cohesion (supports water columns in plant transport cells; surface tension).
- **3.1.8 Inorganic ions** — role dependent on properties; specifically: hydrogen ions and pH; iron ions in haemoglobin; sodium ions in co-transport of glucose/amino acids; phosphate ions in DNA and ATP.

### Topic 2 (spec 3.2) — Cells
- **3.2.1 Cell structure**
  - *3.2.1.1 Eukaryotic cells* — structure and function of: cell-surface membrane, nucleus (chromosomes = protein-bound linear DNA + nucleoli), mitochondria, chloroplasts (plants/algae), Golgi apparatus/vesicles, lysosomes, ribosomes, rough/smooth endoplasmic reticulum, cell wall (plants/algae/fungi), cell vacuole (plants). Specialisation → tissues → organs → systems.
  - *3.2.1.2 Prokaryotic cells and viruses* — prokaryotes: no membrane-bound organelles, smaller ribosomes, single circular DNA free in cytoplasm (not protein-associated), murein cell wall; may have plasmids/capsule/flagella (structural details not required). Viruses: acellular, non-living; genetic material + capsid + attachment protein.
  - *3.2.1.3 Methods of studying cells* — principles/limitations of optical, transmission electron and scanning electron microscopes; magnification vs resolution; $\text{magnification} = \dfrac{\text{size of image}}{\text{size of real object}}$; principles of cell fractionation and ultracentrifugation.
- **3.2.2 All cells arise from other cells** — cell cycle (interphase, mitosis, cytokinesis); DNA replication in interphase; stages of mitosis (prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase) and spindle fibre/centromere role; uncontrolled division → tumours/cancer; binary fission in prokaryotes (circular DNA + plasmid replication); viruses don't undergo cell division (host cell replicates virus particles after nucleic acid injection). Meiosis cross-referenced to topic 4 (spec 3.4.3).
  - **Required practical 2**: stained root-tip squashes; optical microscope identification of mitosis stages; mitotic index calculation. Actual size formula: $\text{actual size} = \dfrac{\text{size of image}}{\text{magnification}}$.
- **3.2.3 Transport across cell membranes** — fluid-mosaic model (phospholipids, proteins, glycoproteins, glycolipids, cholesterol); simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion (carrier/channel proteins), osmosis (water potential), active transport (carrier proteins + ATP hydrolysis), co-transport (Na⁺/glucose absorption in the ileum); surface area and channel/carrier density as rate adaptations.
  - **Required practical 3**: dilution series → calibration curve → water potential of plant tissue.
  - **Required practical 4**: effect of a named variable on cell-surface membrane permeability.
- **3.2.4 Cell recognition and the immune system** — antigens (pathogens, other-organism cells, abnormal body cells, toxins); phagocytosis and lysozyme destruction; cellular response (antigen-presenting cells, helper T cells → cytotoxic T cells/B cells/phagocytes); humoral response (B lymphocytes, clonal selection, antibody structure, antigen-antibody complex → agglutination/phagocytosis, plasma cells vs memory cells, primary/secondary response); vaccines and herd immunity; active vs passive immunity; HIV structure and replication in helper T cells, why antibiotics don't work on viruses; monoclonal antibodies (targeting therapeutic drugs, medical diagnosis — production methods not required); ethical issues; ELISA test.

### Topic 3 (spec 3.3) — Organisms exchange substances with their environment
- **3.3.1 Surface area to volume ratio** — relationship between organism/structure size and SA:V; body-shape/system adaptations as SA:V falls; link to metabolic rate.
- **3.3.2 Gas exchange** — adaptations across: single-celled organism body surface; insect tracheal system (tracheae, tracheoles, spiracles); fish gills (lamellae, filaments, counter-current principle); dicotyledonous leaves (mesophyll, stomata); terrestrial insect/xerophyte compromises (gas exchange vs water loss). Human system: alveoli, bronchioles, bronchi, trachea, lungs; alveolar epithelium; ventilation mechanism (diaphragm, external/internal intercostal muscles). $PVR = \text{tidal volume} \times \text{breathing rate}$. Lung disease/smoking data interpretation; correlation vs causation.
- **3.3.3 Digestion and absorption** — carbohydrate digestion (amylases, membrane-bound disaccharidases); lipid digestion (lipase, bile salts); protein digestion (endopeptidases, exopeptidases, membrane-bound dipeptidases); absorption in the ileum (co-transport of amino acids/monosaccharides; micelles for lipid absorption).
- **3.3.4 Mass transport**
  - *3.3.4.1 In animals* — haemoglobin structure (quaternary) and function; oxyhaemoglobin dissociation curve, cooperative binding, Bohr effect; different haemoglobins across species; mammalian circulation pattern (coronary arteries; vessels to/from heart, lungs, kidneys); heart structure, pressure/volume changes and valve action in the cardiac cycle; artery/arteriole/vein structure vs function; capillaries and capillary beds; tissue fluid formation/return. $CO = \text{stroke volume} \times \text{heart rate}$.
    - **Required practical 5**: dissection of an animal or plant gas exchange/mass transport system, or an organ within one.
  - *3.3.4.2 In plants* — xylem (cohesion-tension theory of water transport); phloem (mass flow hypothesis of translocation); tracer/ringing experiments as evidence.

### Topic 4 (spec 3.4) — Genetic information, variation and relationships between organisms
- **3.4.1 DNA, genes and chromosomes** — prokaryotic DNA (short, circular, no protein); eukaryotic nuclear DNA (long, linear, histone-associated → chromosome); mitochondrial/chloroplast DNA (short, circular, no protein, like prokaryotes); gene = base sequence coding for a polypeptide or functional RNA; locus; triplet code (universal, non-overlapping, degenerate); non-coding DNA; exons vs introns.
- **3.4.2 DNA and protein synthesis** — genome vs proteome; mRNA and tRNA structure; transcription (RNA polymerase); prokaryotes → direct mRNA; eukaryotes → pre-mRNA spliced to mRNA; translation (ribosomes, tRNA, ATP). Specific codon-to-amino-acid recall is **not** required.
- **3.4.3 Genetic diversity from mutation or meiosis** — gene mutations (base deletion/substitution) arising spontaneously in replication; degenerate code means not all substitutions change the amino acid; mutagenic agents; chromosome number mutations via non-disjunction; meiosis (two divisions, diploid → four haploid daughter cells; independent segregation of homologous chromosomes; crossing over).
  - **Required practical 6**: aseptic techniques to investigate effect of antimicrobial substances on microbial growth. *(Numbered here in the spec's genetic-diversity section, alongside natural selection content below.)*
- **3.4.4 Genetic diversity and adaptation** — genetic diversity = number of alleles in a population; principles of natural selection (random mutation → new allele → advantage in certain environments → increased reproductive success → increased frequency over generations); directional selection (antibiotic resistance) vs stabilising selection (human birth weight); anatomical/physiological/behavioural adaptations.
- **3.4.5 Species and taxonomy** — species = able to produce fertile offspring; courtship behaviour; phylogenetic classification hierarchy: domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species; binomial naming. Three-domain/five-kingdom system recall **not** required.
- **3.4.6 Biodiversity within a community** — species richness; index of diversity relates species count to individuals-per-species. *(The spec's own formula extracted with a formatting artefact from the PDF: reconstructed here as $d = \dfrac{N(N-1)}{\sum n(n-1)}$, where $N$ = total organisms of all species and $n$ = organisms of each species — this reconstruction should be cross-checked against the printed formula in your own copy of the spec or data booklet before treating it as exam-ready.)* Farming vs conservation trade-off.
- **3.4.7 Investigating diversity** — genetic diversity comparison via: frequency of observable characteristics, DNA base sequence, mRNA base sequence, amino acid sequence. Gene technology has shifted inference toward direct DNA sequencing (gene technologies themselves not tested here). Quantitative variation: random sampling, mean, standard deviation (interpretation required; **calculating** standard deviation in written papers is not required).

### Topic 5 (spec 3.5) — Energy transfers in and between organisms *(A-level only)*
- **3.5.1 Photosynthesis** — light-dependent reaction: chlorophyll absorption → photoionisation; ATP + reduced NADP production via electron transfer chain and proton gradient across chloroplast membranes (chemiosmotic theory, ATP synthase); photolysis of water → protons + electrons + oxygen. Light-independent reaction (Calvin cycle): CO₂ + RuBP → 2×GP (via rubisco); ATP + reduced NADP reduce GP → triose phosphate; triose phosphate regenerates RuBP or forms organic substances. Limiting factors and agricultural practice.
  - **Required practical 7**: chromatography of leaf pigments.
  - **Required practical 8**: effect of a named factor on dehydrogenase activity in chloroplast extracts.
- **3.5.2 Respiration** — glycolysis (cytoplasm, anaerobic): glucose → glucose phosphate (uses ATP) → triose phosphate → pyruvate (net ATP + reduced NAD gain). Anaerobic route: pyruvate → ethanol or lactate (regenerates oxidised NAD). Aerobic route: pyruvate → mitochondrial matrix (active transport) → oxidised to acetate (+ reduced NAD) → acetylcoenzyme A (with coenzyme A) → Krebs cycle (6C → reduced coenzymes + ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation + CO₂ released) → oxidative phosphorylation (electron transfer chain, proton gradient across inner mitochondrial membrane, ATP synthase, chemiosmotic theory). Lipid/amino acid breakdown products also feed the Krebs cycle.
  - **Required practical 9**: effect of a named variable on respiration rate of single-celled organism cultures.
- **3.5.3 Energy and ecosystems** — gross primary production (GPP); biomass measured as carbon mass or dry mass (calorimetry); $N_{PP} = G_{PP} - R$; net production of consumers $N = I - (F + R)$ where $I$ = energy in ingested food, $F$ = energy lost in faeces/urine, $R$ = respiratory loss; productivity as rate (e.g. kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹); farming efficiency via simplified food webs and reduced respiratory loss.
- **3.5.4 Nutrient cycles** — nitrogen and phosphorus cycles; saprobionts in decomposition; mycorrhizae aiding water/ion uptake; bacterial roles in the nitrogen cycle (saprobiotic nutrition, ammonification, nitrification, nitrogen fixation, denitrification — species names not required); fertiliser use; leaching and eutrophication.

### Topic 6 (spec 3.6) — Organisms respond to changes in their internal and external environments *(A-level only)*
- **3.6.1 Detection and response**
  - *3.6.1.1 Survival and response* — plant growth substances; IAA concentration effects on cell elongation in roots/shoots (gravitropism, phototropism); taxes and kineses; three-neurone simple reflex (spinal cord/root detail not required).
    - **Required practical 10**: effect of an environmental variable on animal movement (choice chamber or maze).
  - *3.6.1.2 Receptors* — Pacinian corpuscle: specific stimulus response, generator potential, stretch-mediated Na⁺ channels; retina: rods/cones, optical pigments, optic nerve connections explaining sensitivity to light/colour and visual acuity.
  - *3.6.1.3 Control of heart rate* — myogenic stimulation; SAN, AVN, bundle of His/Purkyne tissue; chemoreceptors and pressure receptors; autonomic nervous system. $CO = R \times V$ (heart rate × stroke volume).
- **3.6.2 Nervous coordination**
  - *3.6.2.1 Nerve impulses* — myelinated motor neurone structure; resting potential (differential permeability, electrochemical gradients, Na⁺/K⁺ movement); depolarisation and action potential; all-or-nothing principle; myelinated vs non-myelinated conduction; refractory period; speed factors (myelination/saltatory conduction, axon diameter, temperature).
  - *3.6.2.2 Synaptic transmission* — synapse and neuromuscular junction structure; cholinergic synapse transmission sequence (unidirectionality, temporal/spatial summation, inhibition by inhibitory synapses); comparison with neuromuscular junction; drug effects on synapses (specific drug names not required).
- **3.6.3 Skeletal muscle contraction** — antagonistic pairs; gross/microscopic muscle structure; myofibril ultrastructure; actin, myosin, calcium ions, ATP in contraction; tropomyosin role (troponin role not required); phosphocreatine; slow vs fast skeletal muscle fibres.
- **3.6.4 Homeostasis**
  - *3.6.4.1 Principles and negative feedback* — stable core temperature/blood pH (enzyme activity); stable blood glucose (respiratory substrate availability, water potential); negative feedback; separate mechanisms for departures in each direction.
  - *3.6.4.2 Blood glucose control* — liver glycogenesis/glycogenolysis/gluconeogenesis; insulin action (receptor binding, channel protein inclusion, glycogen conversion enzyme activation); glucagon action (glycogen → glucose, glycerol/amino acids → glucose); adrenaline action; second messenger model (adenylate cyclase, cAMP, protein kinase); Type I vs Type II diabetes and control.
    - **Required practical 11**: glucose dilution series → colorimetric calibration curve → unknown "urine" sample concentration.
  - *3.6.4.3 Blood water potential control* — osmoregulation; hypothalamus, posterior pituitary, ADH; nephron structure (glomerular filtrate formation; PCT glucose/water reabsorption; loop of Henle sodium gradient; DCT/collecting duct water reabsorption).

### Topic 7 (spec 3.7) — Genetics, populations, evolution and ecosystems *(A-level only)*
- **3.7.1 Inheritance** — genotype vs phenotype; multiple alleles; dominant/recessive/codominant; homozygous/heterozygous; genetic diagrams for monohybrid/dihybrid crosses, sex-linkage, autosomal linkage, multiple alleles, epistasis; chi-squared ($\chi^2$) test for goodness of fit between observed and expected phenotypic ratios (formula itself not given in this extract — check data booklet).
- **3.7.2 Populations** — gene pool, allele frequency; Hardy–Weinberg principle and its conditions; $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$ where $p$ = frequency of one allele, $q$ = frequency of the other.
- **3.7.3 Speciation** — phenotype variation (genetic + environmental); mutation as primary variation source; meiosis + random fertilisation add variation; natural selection (differential survival/reproduction via predation, disease, competition); stabilising/directional/disruptive selection; allopatric vs sympatric speciation; genetic drift importance in small populations.
- **3.7.4 Populations in ecosystems** — community + abiotic environment = ecosystem; niche; carrying capacity (abiotic factors, inter-/intraspecific competition, predation); population estimation (random quadrats/belt transects for slow-moving/non-motile organisms; mark-release-recapture for motile organisms, with its assumptions); primary succession (pioneer species → climax community); conservation via succession management.
  - **Required practical 12**: effect of a named environmental factor on the distribution of a given species.

### Topic 8 (spec 3.8) — The control of gene expression *(A-level only)*
- **3.8.1 Gene mutations and protein structure** — mutation types: addition, deletion, substitution, inversion, duplication, translocation; spontaneous origin, mutagenic agents; single-triplet changes (degenerate code) vs frame shift.
- **3.8.2 Control of gene expression**
  - *3.8.2.1 Most DNA is not translated* — totipotent cells (early embryo only) vs pluripotent (embryonic) vs multipotent/unipotent (mature mammals, e.g. cardiomyocytes); induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells via transcription factors; stem cells in treating disorders.
  - *3.8.2.2 Regulation of transcription/translation* — transcription factors moving cytoplasm→nucleus; oestrogen initiating transcription; epigenetic control (no DNA base-sequence change) via increased DNA methylation or decreased histone acetylation; epigenetics in disease/cancer; RNA interference (RNAi) inhibiting translation.
  - *3.8.2.3 Gene expression and cancer* — benign vs malignant tumours; tumour suppressor genes and oncogenes; abnormal methylation of these; oestrogen concentration and some breast cancers.
- **3.8.3 Genome projects** — sequencing across organisms including humans; genome→proteome translation easier in simpler organisms (non-coding DNA/regulatory genes complicate this in complex organisms); automated sequencing methods.
- **3.8.4 Gene technologies**
  - *3.8.4.1 Recombinant DNA technology* — DNA fragment production (reverse transcriptase → cDNA; restriction enzymes; "gene machine"); PCR (in vitro amplification) vs transformed host cell culture (in vivo amplification); promoter/terminator regions; restriction endonucleases + ligases + vectors; marker genes (specific genes not required); ethical/financial/social issues; link to gene therapy.
  - *3.8.4.2 DNA differences for identification/diagnosis* — labelled DNA probes and hybridisation; screening for heritable conditions/drug response/health risk; genetic counselling, personalised medicine.
  - *3.8.4.3 Genetic fingerprinting* — variable number tandem repeats (VNTRs); PCR-cloned fragment analysis; gel electrophoresis interpretation; uses in forensics, medical diagnosis, animal/plant breeding.

## 5. Likely mapping to your reported difficult topics

You listed "Units 3, 7, some of 4" as difficult. The specification's own topic numbering (used in "Specification at a glance") runs 1–8 exactly as titled above, which most schools follow directly for their own unit numbers. On that basis, the likely correspondence is:

- **Unit 3 → Topic 3: "Organisms exchange substances with their environment"** (surface area:volume, gas exchange, digestion/absorption, mass transport in animals and plants)
- **Unit 7 → Topic 7: "Genetics, populations, evolution and ecosystems"** (inheritance, Hardy–Weinberg, speciation, populations in ecosystems)
- **Unit 4 (partially) → Topic 4: "Genetic information, variation and relationships between organisms"** (DNA/genes/chromosomes, protein synthesis, meiosis/mutation, natural selection, taxonomy, biodiversity)

**This is a reasonable working assumption, not a confirmed fact** — please confirm against your school's own scheme of work before it drives diagnostic/topic-pack priority in later stages.
